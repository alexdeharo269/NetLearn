## Draft captions

**fig_memory. Subgraph centrality is its order-2 term, and memory follows the network sum of that term.** (a) Squared correlation with subgraph centrality $C_{ii}=(e^{\tilde W})_{ii}$, by degree, of the disparity $Y_i$ alone, the squared normalised strength $\tilde s_i^2$ alone, their product, the order-2 term $\tilde s_i^2Y_i$, and the expansion up to order 3 (weighted triangles); all nodes of the 3,901 connectomes, values in the legend over all nodes. (b) MC against the stable rank $r_s=\sum_i\tilde s_i^2Y_i/0.9801$, development (age ≤ 32 years) and aging. (c) Correlation of each predictor with MC in development and aging. (d) Partial correlation with MC given the stable rank (cubic in $\log r_s$), all connectomes; only the triangles (order 3) add appreciably, and average controllability, the trace of the controllability Gramian (Gu et al. 2015), enters negatively. (e) Change in MC of five null models with respect to the real connectome, 100 age-balanced connectomes (stochastic nulls averaged over 10 realisations, deterministic nulls over 10 input realisations); diamonds, median change predicted from the change in stable rank.

**fig_lifespan_aging. Interhemispheric weight sets the aging course of memory.** (a) MC across the lifespan, means per year of age within each cohort; before adulthood the trajectory compares cohorts (SI Appendix). (b) Within camCAN and HCPa (age ≥ 36), z-scored within each cohort and averaged in age bins (95% CI): memory and subgraph centrality rise, the disparity ratio stays flat and the homotopic share $h$ falls. (c) Correlation with age within each cohort, and of MC controlling for $h$. (d) MC of the connectomes under 40 years when their interhemispheric weights are multiplied by $\kappa$ (simulated; mean and 95% CI); dotted lines, the $\kappa$ that brings their interhemispheric weight fraction to that of participants of 70 or more, whose mean MC is shown by the dashed lines.

**figS_lifespan_full.** As v3 Fig. 3 (panels a and b from v1 Fig. 4: disparity against degree, and the disparity ratio across the lifespan), with the virtual aging simulated.

**figS_development. Development, within cohorts.** (a) Correlation with age, within each cohort (95% CI), of MC and of $h$. (b) MC against age within camCAN (single site), with 5-year bin means (95% CI) and the turning point of a piecewise-linear fit (35 years; bootstrap 95% CI 30–55, shaded). (c, d) MC and $h$ of HCPya and camCAN participants of the same ages (22–37 years).

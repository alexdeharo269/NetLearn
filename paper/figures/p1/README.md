## Captions

**Fig. 3. Lifespan reorganisation of weight disparity and its structural correlates.** Top, against degree; bottom, across the lifespan. (a) Nodal disparity $\Upsilon=kY$ (points, nodes of a random subsample of connectomes), null-model mean and $\pm2\sigma$ envelope limited to $1\le\Upsilon\le k$ (grey), fully heterogeneous limit $\Upsilon=k$ (dashed red) and degree-binned mean over all connectomes, $\propto k^{0.35}$ (blue). (b) Share of $C_{ii}-1$ captured by the walk expansion truncated at orders 2 to 5 (mean over nodes; interquartile range for orders 2 and 2-3). (c) Share of a region's squared weight carried by its homotopic connection, $h_i=w_{ii'}^2/\sum_jw_{ij}^2$ (mean and 95% CI over nodes), against the $1/k$ expected if the weights of a region were exchangeable. (d-f) Per-age means of each cohort of the disparity ratio, subgraph centrality and homotopic share $h$.

**Fig. 5. Reservoir memory across the lifespan and its structural predictors.** (a) MC against age (grey points) with a linear fit in development (age at most 32 years, the minimum of the running mean of MC) and in aging; dashed red, linear fits of the disparity ratio (right axis). (b) MC against the stable rank $r_s$, by regime. (c) Correlation with MC in development and aging of the stable rank, subgraph centrality, communicability, the disparity ratio, strength, degree and average controllability (trace of the controllability Gramian). (d) Partial correlation with MC given the stable rank (cubic in $\log r_s$), all connectomes. (e) Change in MC of five null models with respect to the real connectome, 100 age-balanced connectomes: uniform weights, broken-stick weights, weights reshuffled among the connections (10 realisations each), interhemispheric weights halved, and interhemispheric weight fraction set to the pooled mean, 0.29 (both over 10 input realisations); diamonds, median change predicted from the change in stable rank. (f) The 15 strongest connections at three stages of life (share of the squared weight, averaged over the subjects of each cohort and then over cohorts): newborns (dHCP), 30-40 years (HCPya, camCAN, HCPa) and 76 years or more (camCAN, HCPa), drawn at the centroids of their AAL regions (axial view, hemispheres pulled apart), line width proportional to the share; green, between the hemispheres; brown, within a hemisphere. Below, the share of the squared weight between the hemispheres and the mean MC. The thickest connection at 30-40 years joins the left and right supplementary motor areas. (g) MC against age in the aging cohorts (camCAN and HCPa, older than 32 years), linear fits, for the real connectomes, with the interhemispheric weight equalised (interhemispheric weight fraction of every connectome set to the pooled mean, 0.29) and with only the homotopic weight equalised (homotopic share of the squared weight set to the pooled mean, 0.34).

Colours: blue, disparity, order 2 and even walks; orange, triangles and odd walks; green, interhemispheric; brown, intrahemispheric; red, disparity ratio; grey, null models; yellow and purple, development and aging.

**figS_development. Development, within cohorts.** (a) Correlation with age, within each cohort (95% CI), of MC and of $h$. (b) MC against age within camCAN (single site), with 5-year bin means (95% CI) and the turning point of a piecewise-linear fit (35 years; bootstrap 95% CI 30-55, shaded). (c, d) MC and $h$ of HCPya and camCAN participants of the same ages (22-37 years).

**figS_walks. Closed walks beyond the stable rank.** Correlation with MC of closed walks of length $n$ ($\log\mathrm{Tr}\,\tilde W^n$, all connectomes; filled, $n=2$ is the stable rank) and partial correlation given the stable rank (open; cubic in $\log r_s$). Right, a closed walk of even length can go back and forth along the strongest connection; one of odd length must close a triangle or another odd cycle. Given the stable rank, odd lengths add and even lengths subtract.

## Numbers (`paper/data/p1/numbers_p1.json`)

- Walk expansion of $C_{ii}$, $R^2$ over all nodes: $Y_i$ 0.006; $\tilde s_i^2$ 0.71; order 2 0.9993; orders 2-3 0.9997. Order 3 adds 1-2% of $C_{ii}-1$ below degree 10 and about 6% above degree 40.
- $\langle\Upsilon\rangle_k$ above the null mean for $k\ge4$ and above its $2\sigma$ envelope for $k\ge12$; exponent 0.35.
- Homotopic connection: 19% of a region's squared weight at degrees 18-45, against 4% for exchangeable weights.
- MC vs log stable rank: $r=0.95$ (development 0.95, aging 0.93; within cohorts median 0.89, 0.82-0.94). Given the stable rank, age no longer predicts MC (development -0.04, aging -0.03).
- Nulls (median change, observed / predicted): Uniform -1.89 / -1.94; Broken stick -0.79 / -1.17; Reshuffle +0.05 / +0.08; interhemispheric halved +1.07 / +1.05; interhemispheric equalised +0.32 / +0.29.
- MC vs h: $r=-0.70$ (development -0.75, aging -0.65).
- The strongest interhemispheric connection carries 5% of the squared weight in newborns, 36% at 21-37 years and 7% after 75; the strongest intrahemispheric one 8.5%, 3% and 12%. Within camCAN and HCPa the first decreases with age (r = -0.49, -0.45) and the second increases (0.39, 0.46). r(MC, strongest interhemispheric share) = -0.83.
- Walk lengths, r with MC (development / aging): n=4 0.79 / 0.72; n=5 0.87 / 0.82; n=6 0.37 / 0.28; n=7 0.85 / 0.79; n=8 -0.05 / -0.08. Given the stable rank: n=3 +0.33, 4 -0.25, 5 +0.43, 6 -0.37, 7 +0.46, 8 -0.45.
- Interhemispheric weight equalised, r(age, MC) within cohorts: development -0.03 -> -0.05; aging 0.29 -> 0.13.
- Interhemispheric weight equalised (simulated): correlation of MC with age within camCAN 0.36 -> 0.09, within HCPa 0.22 -> 0.09; developmental cohorts near 0 before and after. Equalising only the homotopic connections does not remove it (0.26, 0.28).
- With age, the strongest intrahemispheric connection (superior-middle frontal, SMA-putamen, postcentral-putamen) gains share of the squared weight (r = 0.37 camCAN, 0.46 HCPa; 5.4% at 33-44 years, 12.1% at 75-90).
- Three stages (equal weight per cohort): share of the squared weight between the hemispheres 18% (newborns), 65% (30-40 years), 18% (76 or more); MC 11.5, 9.6, 10.7. At 30-40 years the left-right supplementary motor area connection carries 27% of the squared weight (HCPa 20%, HCPya 37%, camCAN 25%) and is the strongest connection in 90% of subjects; in newborns the strongest is Precentral_R-Putamen_R (3.2%); from 76 years, Supp_Motor_Area_L-Supp_Motor_Area_R (4.5%, the strongest in 14% of subjects).
